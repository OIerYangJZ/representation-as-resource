\noindent
Quantum circuit lowering is usually treated as a semantics-preserving translation: high-level
structure is decomposed into a fixed native basis, and downstream passes optimize the resulting
instruction stream. We ask a sharper question. \emph{When lowering preserves the unitary, does it
also preserve the recoverability of an optimization opportunity for a resource-bounded compiler?}
The answer is no. We exhibit a Fourier-layer family on which semantic-first compilation compresses
repeated commuting-diagonal structure to growth in the number of phase terms alone, while any
compiler below a precise resource threshold retains growth in the repetition count as well. The
phenomenon is not inverse cancellation, pass ordering, or a claim that one named tool dominates
another; it is a representation-level information bottleneck.

\section*{The witness and the separation}

Let $D_m(\theta)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\exp(-i\theta_j P_j/2)$, where the $P_j$ are bounded-arity diagonal
phase terms that all commute, and define the non-inverse family
\[
  C_{m,r} \;=\; H_\Lambda\, D_m(\theta)^{\,r}\, H_\Lambda ,
\]
with $H_\Lambda$ a fixed Hadamard boundary. There is no adjacent $UU^\dagger$ cancellation; the only
algebraic opportunity is that the diagonal factors commute, so repeated coefficients can be added
\emph{before} basis materialization. Writing $\mathrm{pg}_B(\cdot)$ for the number of nontrivial
phase-gadget atoms retained after lowering to a fixed basis $B$, our main result is a
resource-bounded separation.

\begin{theorem}[Fourier-layer recoverability separation]
Fix resource budgets and a bounded-arity lowering scheme. For $C_{m,r}$ with $m>w$ under bounded
arity, non-resonance, and periodic separated lowering, every compiler that does \emph{not}
instantiate diagonal aggregation $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{diag}}$ satisfies
$\mathrm{pg}_B\big(A(L_B(C_{m,r}))\big)\ge c\,rm-O(m)$,
while a semantic compiler that aggregates coefficients before lowering emits
$\mathrm{pg}_B\le C\,m$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed width]
If the width is fixed to a constant $m_0>w$, then $C_{m_0,r}$ gives a direct $\Omega(r)$ versus
$O(1)$ separation: every below-threshold compiler retains $\Omega(r)$ phase-gadget structure, while a
pre-basis semantic compiler emits a constant-size circuit. This is the asymptotic form of the
experiment below, where the semantic artifact emits exactly $42$ gates at every tested scale,
independent of the repetition count $r$.
\end{corollary}

\noindent
The resource $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{diag}}$ is defined as a \emph{capability} rather than a budget
region: a compiler instantiates it on $C_{m,r}$ if, at some point in execution, it constructs data
sufficient to (i) associate each lowered occurrence with its semantic role across repetitions,
(ii) compute or represent the aggregate coefficient $r\theta_j \bmod 2\pi$, and (iii) justify
replacing the $r$ repeated occurrences of each term by $O(1)$ aggregate representatives. ``Below the
threshold'' simply means the execution does none of these. This capability formulation removes any
circular feasible-region object and lets the lower bound be stated as an information obstruction:
after periodic lowering, all interior copies of a phase term look identical to bounded local
observation, so a compiler that has not instantiated $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{diag}}$ cannot group them.
A companion result closes the boundary from the other side.

\begin{theorem}[Necessity of the aggregation resource]
Any semantics-preserving compiler---with no restriction on its internal algorithm---that emits an
$O(m)$ target-basis circuit for $C_{m,r}$ over an unbounded range of $r$ must instantiate
$\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{diag}}$: it must preserve, summarize, lift, or reconstruct an equivalent global
diagonal representation.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
Thus a future tool that recovers the bounded form by building a global phase-polynomial, ZX,
stabilizer-phase, or commuting-diagonal representation would \emph{confirm} the classification rather
than refute it. The theorem characterizes representations, not brands.

\section*{Why the witness is natural}

The family is synthetic in form but natural in source. Under standard decompositions, the QFT, the
approximate QFT, and quantum phase estimation all contain commuting diagonal phase layers of the
form $D_m(\theta)$ adjacent to a Fourier or Hadamard basis-change boundary. The witness isolates
exactly this object between fixed Hadamard boundaries and repeats it, so that the mechanism under
test---coefficient addition in a commuting diagonal representation---is not confounded by routing,
terminal swaps, inverse cancellation, or approximation thresholds. We do not claim every QFT or QPE
instance exhibits the same gap; the claim is narrower and stronger: the diagonal layer is a genuine
algorithmic object, and a controlled family built from it makes representation alone create the
separation.

\section*{Construction and experimental validation}

We instantiate the resource-rich side in a bounded compiler artifact built on UCC. Structured
circuits are lifted into a small semantic term algebra
($T ::= \mathrm{Prim} \mid T_1\!\circ\! T_2 \mid T^r \mid \mathrm{Conj} \mid \mathrm{Fourier} \mid
\mathrm{Mirror}$), candidate selection is performed on projected costs, and only the winning
candidate is materialized late into the target basis. Keeping the commuting diagonal term explicit
long enough to add coefficients is the implementation-level way the artifact crosses
$\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{diag}}$.

On the fixed-width witness ($n=4$), the semantic path returns a constant $42$-gate circuit (depth
$24$, $12$ two-qubit gates) at every tested scale, while a strong flat preset grows and then times
out (Table~\ref{tab:headline}). A width sweep confirms the predicted $O(m)$ scaling: outputs of
$42$, $65$, and $93$ gates for $n=4,5,6$, constant in $r$ at each width. Crucially, a stripped-down
phase-polynomial reference---no UCC internals, only commuting-diagonal coefficient aggregation on
the same input and target basis---returns the \emph{same} $42$-gate output, demonstrating that the
constant form is a property of the resource, not the tool.

Three further controls tie the result to the predicted mechanism. A Fourier-path \emph{ablation}
disables the semantic lifting and reruns the witness: the constant-output recovery disappears and
the compiler times out at every scale, confirming the $42$-gate form is caused by semantic
aggregation rather than a favorable ordering of flat passes. \emph{External bridge pipelines} built
on PyZX and TKET, evaluated through the same basis protocol, fail to recover the bounded forms,
evidence that in these configured pipelines strong external tools did not reconstruct the global
diagonal representation the necessity theorem identifies as required. Finally,
a Clifford$+T$ \emph{resource proxy} (Table~\ref{tab:resource}) shows the fault-tolerant
consequence: delaying aggregation until after materialization presents a resource estimator with a
linearly growing number of independent rotation-approximation tasks, whereas the resource-rich path
holds at a constant count. On official QFT/QPE/QAOA-family instances the artifact matches the strong
preset while cutting $65$--$78\%$ of baseline gate count.

\section*{Significance}

The result reframes a class of compiler regressions as a representation-resource boundary rather
than a tuning failure: any successful future flat-looking compiler must explicitly or implicitly pay
for the same global aggregation resource. The boundary is falsifiable, and it carries directly into
fault-tolerant resource estimation, where delaying aggregation until after basis materialization
inflates the number of rotations a synthesis backend must approximate. A full technical version,
including all definitions, proofs, and the complete experimental suite, is provided as an appendix.
