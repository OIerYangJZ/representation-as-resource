\section*{1. Motivation and Context}

Quantum circuit lowering---the projection of high-level structure into a fixed hardware-native
basis---is conventionally treated as a semantics-preserving translation. This view conflates unitary
equivalence with structural conservation. Lowering preserves the unitary, but it need not preserve
the \emph{recoverability} of an optimization opportunity for a resource-bounded compiler. Before
lowering, a commuting diagonal phase layer names its generators and coefficients explicitly, so
repeated layers aggregate by adding coefficients. After lowering, the same information is spread
across many locally similar fragments, and a compiler limited in passes, scanning window, working
memory, and persistent metadata faces an information bottleneck: it cannot recognize the fragments
as members of one global aggregate without spending enough summary or lifting resource.

This is not synthetic. Fourier-based subroutines---QFT, AQFT, QPE, amplitude estimation---naturally
contain commuting diagonal phase layers $D_m(\theta)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\exp(-i\theta_jP_j/2)$ sandwiched
between Hadamard or Fourier basis-change boundaries. We formalize the resulting boundary, prove it is
an asymptotic separation rather than a pass-ordering artifact, and validate it experimentally.

\section*{2. Main Contributions}

\noindent\textbf{(i) A resource-bounded model of recoverability.} We introduce a six-parameter
capability class $\mathrm{RB}(p,w,s,b,q,\lambda)$ over passes, sliding-window radius, ephemeral
state, persistent metadata, lifted rewrites, and total lifted volume, yielding a hierarchy
$\mathrm{LF}\subseteq\mathrm{SF}\subseteq\mathrm{SAF}\subseteq\mathrm{HYB}\subseteq\mathrm{SEM}$.
Within it we isolate a witness-specific \emph{capability}, diagonal aggregation
$\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{diag}}$: a compiler instantiates it if it constructs data sufficient to
associate each lowered occurrence with its semantic role across repetitions, represent the aggregate
coefficient $r\theta_j\bmod 2\pi$, and justify replacing the $r$ repetitions of each non-removable
$P_j$ by $O(1)$ representatives.

\noindent\textbf{(ii) A Fourier-layer separation theorem.} For the non-inverse family
$C_{m,r}:=H_\Lambda D_m(\theta)^{r}H_\Lambda$ with bounded-arity $P_j$, non-resonance, and periodic
separated lowering, every compiler below the threshold,
$A\in\mathrm{RB}^{<\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{diag}}}$, retains
$\mathrm{pg}_B(A(L_B(C_{m,r})))\ge c\,rm-O(m)$ bounded-arity phase-gadget atoms, while a compiler that
aggregates before lowering emits $\mathrm{pg}_B\le C\,m$. At fixed width $m=m_0>w$ this is an
$\Omega(r)$ versus $O(1)$ separation.

\noindent\textbf{(iii) A necessity theorem.} Under independence of the diagonal characters, any
semantics-preserving compiler---regardless of internal algorithm---that achieves the $O(m)$ scaling
for unbounded $r$ must instantiate $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{diag}}$, explicitly (a global
phase-polynomial, ZX, stabilizer-phase, or commuting-diagonal IR) or implicitly (an equivalent
algebraic rewrite). This is a \emph{classification}, not a universal impossibility: a future
flat-looking compiler that recovers the bounded form is thereby shown to have funneled resource into
one of these channels.

\noindent\textbf{(iv) Tool-independent instantiation and validation.} We realize the resource-rich
side two ways: a bounded semantic-UCC artifact over a term algebra
($T ::= \mathrm{Prim}\mid T_1\!\circ\!T_2\mid T^r\mid \mathrm{Conj}\mid \mathrm{Fourier}\mid
\mathrm{Mirror}$) with late materialization, and a minimal phase-polynomial reference that only
aggregates the commuting \texttt{rz}/\texttt{cp} coefficients. On the $n=4$ fixed-width witness both
return a constant $42$-gate, depth-$24$, $12$-\texttt{cx} circuit from $4$k to $100$k input gates,
while \texttt{qiskit opt3}, a PyZX bridge, and TKET FullPeephole grow or time out
(Table~\ref{tab:headline}). A width sweep gives $42,65,93$ gates for $n=4,5,6$, confirming the
$O(m)$ law with $m=n(n-1)/2$; all $45$ configurations are certified by exact unitary equivalence
with zero variance across nonresonant phase seeds.

\section*{3. Techniques}

The lower bound rests on a local-profile multiplicity lemma. Under periodic separated lowering,
interior copies of the same semantic role $P_j$ induce radius-$w$ neighborhoods identical up to
translation outside an $O(1)$ prefix/suffix anomaly zone. For any compiler below
$\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{diag}}$, the accessible profile of an interior position has multiplicity
$\Theta(r)$; collapsing a constant fraction of those copies into $O(1)$ representatives requires
building the role-to-occurrence map and the aggregate coefficient---that is, instantiating
$\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{diag}}$. This is an information bottleneck rather than a circuit-complexity
bound: enough persistent metadata or lifted volume removes the obstruction and moves the compiler up
the hierarchy. The upper bound is the Abelian aggregation identity
$\big(\prod_j e^{-i\theta_jP_j/2}\big)^r=\prod_j e^{-i(r\theta_j)P_j/2}$; the necessity theorem runs
it in reverse, using independence of the characters to fix $(r\theta_1,\dots,r\theta_m)\bmod2\pi$ up
to global phase. A Fourier-path ablation isolates causality: disabling the semantic IR forces
timeout at every scale, while Fourier-enabled paths deterministically recover the $42$-gate form,
and minimal pass-reordering repairs fail to close the gap ($485{,}780$ vs.\ $166{,}805$ gates on
\texttt{phase\_estimation\_real}).

\section*{4. Significance}

\noindent\emph{Fault-tolerant resource estimation.} Using the Ross--Selinger synthesis scaling
$T_\epsilon(R)=\lceil 3\log_2(1/\epsilon)\rceil R$ as a proxy, semantic-first compilation holds the
fixed-width witness at $22$ rotations and a constant $T_{10^{-10}}$ proxy of $2{,}200$, whereas
materialize-first \texttt{qiskit opt3} reaches a proxy of $2{,}399{,}300$ at $20$k before timing out.
Delayed aggregation therefore inflates the rotation multiplicity that feeds magic-state and T-count
estimates---an $\Omega(r)$ effect on downstream distillation demand.

\noindent\emph{Architecture.} The result reclassifies semantic term layers, projected-cost selection,
and delayed materialization from arbitrary engineering choices into the mechanism by which a compiler
crosses the threshold. The boundary is falsifiable: a pipeline recovering the bounded form without
any equivalent global representation would break the threshold, while recovery via global
reconstruction confirms the classification. The minimal $HD^rH$ witness strips away routing and
approximation noise, serving as a precise probe of this representation boundary.

\section*{5. Relation to Prior Work}

The separation isolates non-inverse, non-local Abelian phase aggregation, distinguishing it from
local $UU^\dagger$ inverse-cancellation frameworks. It also offers a common lens on several advanced
compilers: phase-polynomial optimization (Nam et al.) builds an aggregate diagonal object before
re-synthesis; ZX-calculus rewriting (Duncan et al.; PyZX) operates directly on a global semantic
representation; and lazy synthesis (Martiel--Goubault de Brugi\`ere) defers premature lowering. The
necessity theorem frames these as different routes to instantiating $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{diag}}$
rather than unrelated tools. Unlike full-stack benchmarks (MQT Bench, SupermarQ), $HD^rH$ is an
isolated theorem witness targeting the commuting-diagonal topology native to QFT, AQFT, and QPE,
without conflating the result with routing constraints or approximate-synthesis noise. A full
technical version with all definitions, proofs, and the complete experimental suite is provided as an
appendix.
