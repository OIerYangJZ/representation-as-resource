Representation as a Computational Resource in Quantum Compilation

Approximate recoverability tradeoffs and model-specific fault-tolerant consequences

Jinze Yang1 · Yangyang Li1 · Xiu-Hao Deng2,3
1 Xidian University · 2 Shenzhen International Quantum Academy · 3 Hefei National Laboratory

01  Compilation destroys structure

Fault-tolerant pipelines deliberately scatter a quantity that was once written in one place — and the scattering is correct, standard, and desirable in each case:

  • Scheduling. Variational and time-dependent layers split one accumulated phase across many rounds.
  • Synthesis. An angle becomes a Clifford+T word in which no single gate carries the angle.
  • Randomized compiling. Each rotation is twirled into sign-randomized fragments.

We ask what it costs a downstream compiler — a cloud transpiler, a control-stack stream optimizer — to put the quantity back together, and show the price is payable in exactly two currencies: bits of compiler state, or magic states.

02  A budget-explicit model

Every channel that would let a restarted compiler recover the aggregate is charged in bits — no gate count is ever converted into a bit value:

Recovery is certified, not asserted. The representation-aware pipeline uses bounded recognition, aggregation certificates and conservative fallback; a measured cell becomes numerical only once a symbolic or dense certificate returns completed_valid. Unsupported is never conflated with incorrect, and bridge loss is reported apart from compiler quality.

03  The frontier

Theorem
Ap + (2p−1) S ≥ (1−δ) m log2⁡ Kε − h2⁡ (δ)
for any compiler making p forward passes and failing with probability at most δ

Ap is committed output, S the serialized crossing state, and Kε=Θ(1/ε) the number of distinguishable settings on the Ross–Selinger synthesis grid. The two cannot both be small. The bound is carried by the schedule alone, so the folded compile-time presentation of randomized compiling inherits it verbatim — nothing depends on tracking Pauli frames.

04  Not at par

Once a committed record is executed rather than buffered, the committed channel pays a further log2⁡(mk) — the entropy of the compiler's own decision about which coordinates to commit early. A compiler that defers is handed that decision for free by the stream that follows.

05  What it costs downstream

Propagating one fixed total error budget through actual Clifford+T synthesis and surface-code scheduling turns the representational gap into a fault-tolerant one. Over all 96 matched target / error / allocation / QEC / factory settings, no reversal occurs.

The exchange rate, in miniature

One QAOA cost layer, 3-regular MaxCut on 22 vertices — m=33 edges at ε=10−3. The layer is worth 252 bits, about 31 bytes. A compiler that defers carries those 31 bytes across every cut. One that commits early buys the same information back at roughly 30 T gates per corrected coordinate: order 103 magic states.

06  Where the bound bites

Two hypotheses hold

Variational and Trotter cost layers: rounds contiguous in the stream, coefficients free to vary round to round.

One fails

Controlled-power ladders and phase estimation: contiguous, but round one already fixes the aggregate.

Clifford records — routing, swap, and the Pauli-frame insertions of randomized compiling itself — sit interior to the block and normalize away, so the sign-randomized and folded presentations obey the same floor.

Takeaway
Randomize and lower late.

The information a compiler discards before aggregating cannot be recovered for free — it must be bought back in memory or in magic states, and buying it back early is strictly the more expensive of the two. Treat recoverability as a controlled variable in compilation and resource estimation.
